## Reddit r/SideProject
Title: I analyzed all 14,700 US nursing homes after the Senate started investigating A Place for Mom. The data is worse than I expected.

The Senate is probing A Place for Mom for steering families to facilities that pay commissions — WaPo found a third of its "Best of" winners had care violations. That made me wonder what the actual federal data shows.

So I pulled the full CMS dataset (May 2026 release, every certified facility in America) and ran the numbers:

- 19.6% of US nursing homes are rated 1 star — the lowest possible rating
- 40.1% sit in the bottom two tiers
- 1,482 facilities (1 in 10) carry an active CMS abuse flag
- Facilities have paid $462M in federal fines; 45% have been fined at least once
- Worst state: Missouri, where 1 in 3 facilities is rated 1 star. In Illinois, 29.5% of facilities carry an abuse flag.

Full report with all 50 states ranked (free, no signup): https://yejuntak.github.io/elderpath/report/

This is the first piece of ElderPath — I'm building report cards for senior-living communities from public inspection data, because the referral sites families actually use are paid by the facilities they recommend. Feedback very welcome, especially if you've done this search for a parent.
