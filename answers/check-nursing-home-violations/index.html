How do I check a nursing home's violations and inspection records?

Every Medicare-certified US nursing home's inspection results, violations, fines, and abuse citations are public. The fastest way: search the facility in a free lookup built on the federal CMS dataset, or go directly to Medicare.gov Care Compare. Both show the official record — no referral site needed.

Based on the CMS Provider Information dataset, May 2026 release · Updated June 10, 2026

Where do these records come from?

Every Medicare- and Medicaid-certified nursing home in America is inspected by state surveyors, and the results flow into one federal dataset published by CMS. It includes star ratings, health deficiencies, federal fines, payment denials, staffing hours, and abuse citations — updated monthly. The May 2026 release covers 14,700 facilities.

The two-minute check, step by step

  • Search the facility in the free ElderPath lookup — type the name, city, or state and the federal record renders instantly.
  • Read four numbers: star rating (1–5), abuse flag (yes/no), total fines, and deficiencies vs the 9.3 national average.
  • Verify on the official source — Medicare Care Compare — before any decision, since records update monthly.

What counts as a red flag?

From our analysis of all 14,700 facilities: 19.6% are rated 1 star, 1 in 10 carries an active abuse flag, and 45% have been fined at least once ($462M total). A single old paperwork citation isn't alarming; a low rating combined with an abuse flag or six-figure fines is. One Texas example we found: a 1-star facility with an active abuse flag and $768K in fines across six penalties.

Why doesn't the referral site I used show any of this?

Because most are paid by facilities — up to a month's rent per placement — and showing violation histories works against that model. The Senate Aging Committee opened an investigation into the largest platform in June 2024. The public data above is how you route around the conflict of interest.
