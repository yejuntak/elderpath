What does a 1-star CMS nursing home rating actually mean?

A 1-star CMS rating means a facility ranks in the bottom tier of the federal Five-Star Quality Rating System — driven mostly by inspection results, staffing levels, and quality measures. 19.6% of US nursing homes hold a 1-star overall rating in the May 2026 data; in Missouri it's 1 in 3.

Based on the CMS Provider Information dataset, May 2026 release · Updated June 10, 2026

What goes into the star rating?

The CMS Five-Star system combines three things: health inspection results (the dominant factor — real surveyor findings from on-site visits), staffing (nurse hours per resident per day), and quality measures the facility reports. Because inspections dominate, the stars broadly track what inspectors actually found.

How bad is the national picture?

Worse than most families expect. In the May 2026 dataset of all 14,700 certified facilities: 19.6% hold a 1-star overall rating and 40.1% sit in the bottom two tiers. 1,482 facilities carry an active abuse flag. Facilities have paid $462M in federal fines. Full state-by-state numbers are in our 2026 Transparency Report.

Which states rank worst?

Missouri (34.7% of facilities 1-star), Illinois (33.9%, and 29.5% with an abuse flag — the highest big-state rate), and Louisiana (30.8%). Best: Utah, Hawaii, and Arizona, all near 6–7%. Texas, the largest market, runs 24.2% — and 70% of Texas facilities have been fined.

How should a family actually use the stars?

As a filter, not a verdict. Shortlist 3–4 stars and up, then read each facility's actual record — abuse flag, fines, deficiency count — in the free lookup, and verify on Care Compare. A mediocre rating with clean recent inspections can beat a 5-star with a fresh citation.
