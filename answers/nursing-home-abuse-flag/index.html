What is the CMS abuse flag on a nursing home?

The abuse flag (a red hand icon on Medicare.gov) marks facilities cited for abuse that harmed a resident within the past year, or that could have harmed residents in each of the last two years. In the May 2026 federal data, 1,482 US facilities — 1 in 10 — carry it.

Based on the CMS Provider Information dataset, May 2026 release · Updated June 10, 2026

How a facility earns the flag

This is not a subjective review. CMS attaches the icon only when state inspectors cited the facility for abuse that led to harm in the past year, or for abuse with potential for harm in each of the last two years. It appears on the facility's federal record and on Medicare.gov as a red hand icon.

How common is it?

1,482 facilities — 1 in 10 nationally — carry the flag in the May 2026 data. The variation is striking: 29.5% of Illinois facilities carry it, roughly 25% in Colorado and Arizona, under 2% in Nebraska and Maine. State-by-state figures are in the 2026 report.

What should a family do with this information?

  • Check before you tour: the free lookup shows the flag instantly (try the browse chip "Abuse-flagged" filtered to your state).
  • Look at the whole record: rating, fines, and deficiency count together tell you whether the flag is part of a pattern.
  • Ask the administrator directly what was cited and what changed — a good facility will answer in specifics.
  • Verify on Care Compare, and report suspected abuse to your state's long-term-care ombudsman.
