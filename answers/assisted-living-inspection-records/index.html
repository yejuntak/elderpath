Are assisted living facilities inspected like nursing homes?

Yes — but by 50 different state agencies, not the federal government. Unlike nursing homes, assisted living has no national dataset, no star ratings, and no abuse flags. Records exist on each state's licensing website, usually as PDFs, which is why most families never see them.

Based on the CMS Provider Information dataset, May 2026 release · Updated June 10, 2026

The short answer: inspected yes, transparent no

Assisted-living communities are licensed and inspected by state agencies, and citations are public record. But there's no federal dataset, no star rating, no abuse flag, and no single place to search. The records live on 50 different state portals — Texas HHS's Long-Term Care search, California DSS, Florida AHCA — mostly as scanned PDFs.

Why this gap matters

For nursing homes, we could analyze all 14,700 facilities in an afternoon because CMS publishes everything (here's that analysis). For assisted living — where pricing is opaque and referral platforms are paid by facilities — families have less safety data at exactly the decision point with less regulation. The Senate's 2024 probe of the largest referral platform found over a third of its award-winning facilities had care violations on record.

How to check a facility today, state by state

  • Search "[your state] assisted living licensing search" — the official portal is usually the first .gov result.
  • Pull the facility's full inspection history, not just the latest visit; look for repeat citations and whether corrections were verified.
  • Ask the community for its last two inspection reports in writing — licensed facilities have them.
  • For nursing-home-level care, use the federal-data lookup — that part is already solved.
